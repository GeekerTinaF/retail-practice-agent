# Evidence Policy

These rules apply to every fact, number and claim in every retail-research
output.

## 1. Source hierarchy

Use the highest tier available.

| Tier | Type | Examples | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary company sources | IR releases, annual reports, investor presentations, newsroom, regulatory filings, careers pages (a signal for expansion or automation hiring) | Company facts |
| 1 | Official statistics | Destatis, Eurostat, European Commission, Bundesbank, national statistics offices | Market quantities |
| 2 | Strategic research | McKinsey (e.g. *State of Fashion*), Deloitte (e.g. *Global Powers of Retailing/Luxury*) | Framing and trends (see §2) |
| 2 | Industry associations | HDE, BTE, bevh, EHI, and equivalents in other countries | Sector figures; always state the association's own definition |
| 3 | Trade press | Retail Detail, Retail Week, Just Style, Logistics Manager, DC Velocity, LogisticsIT, TextilWirtschaft | Sector news, with named sources |
| 4 | General business press | Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, Handelsblatt | Corporate events |
| 5 | Secondary commentary | Aggregators, analyst blogs, LinkedIn, AI summaries, Wikipedia | Finding leads only |

**Rules**

- Tier 5 is never cited as a standalone fact.
- If a tier-5 site summarises a tier-1 document (e.g. an SEC filing), cite
  the tier-1 document. Mark it "via aggregator" and treat anything the
  summary adds as Unconfirmed.
- If only a low-tier source exists, say so explicitly and label the finding
  Unconfirmed.
- If a source is paywalled or could not be retrieved, cite only the parts
  actually seen, and say that access was limited.

## 2. McKinsey and Deloitte

- Always cite the exact report title and year. Never write "McKinsey says"
  without naming the report.
- Label forward-looking statements (predictions, "retailers should…") as
  **Forecast** or **Analysis**, never as Reported.
- Use these reports for market framing. Do not use them as evidence about a
  company's internal operations, unless the report names that company.

## 3. Company information

Use sources in this order:

1. The company's own IR site, annual report or press room.
2. Regulatory filings (company registries, stock exchange or SEC filings).
3. Direct company statements quoted in reputable press.
4. Trade press reporting with named sources.

Wikipedia, aggregator databases and AI summaries may only point to where a
primary source can be found.

## 4. Evidence labels

Every claim carries exactly one label.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reported** | A figure or fact as published by the named source. |
| **Calculated** | Our arithmetic on Reported data. The method must be stated, e.g. "mean of 12 monthly values". |
| **Forecast** | A projection or estimate made by the named source. |
| **Analysis** | Our interpretation. It is never a fact and never a customer requirement. |
| **Unconfirmed** | Could not be verified against a tier 1–3 source. |

Fact and interpretation must be visually and textually separable. A sentence
must not mix a Reported figure with an Analysis conclusion without labelling
both.

## 5. Traceability requirements

Every number must carry:

- value and unit;
- period;
- geography;
- source organisation, title, publication date and URL;
- access date, where the source can change (web pages, databases).

Also record the research date for every output. If the publication date
could not be captured, write "date not captured". Do not guess it.

## 6. Definitions and comparability

- State the metric definition for every quantitative series (nominal vs
  real; seasonal adjustment; index base; population covered).
- Never combine sources with different definitions in one number or chart.
  If they must be compared, show them side by side and explain the
  difference. Examples of incompatible pairs:
  - HICP vs national CPI;
  - EU consumer confidence vs GfK consumer climate;
  - bevh vs BTE market definitions.
- Flag structural breaks and known statistical effects, e.g. a
  reclassification of a large company.

## 7. Conflicting sources

- Show both values, each with its source.
- Name the more authoritative source according to §1.
- If the conflict has a known explanation (reported vs constant currency,
  headcount at different dates), state it. If it does not, leave the
  conflict unresolved and add it to the evidence-gap register.

## 8. Evidence gaps

- Never estimate, infer or invent to fill a gap.
- Every output ends with a consolidated **evidence-gap register**. For each
  gap, record:
  - what is missing;
  - why it matters;
  - where it might be found;
  - whether it has become a watchlist item.
- If a KPI or chart cannot be supported, omit it and record the omission.
  Do not substitute a weaker proxy without saying so.

## 9. Relationship claims

- Do not claim or imply an existing Geek+ relationship with any account,
  unless a primary or Geek+-internal source verifies it.
- A public Geek+ reference case may be cited as capability evidence. State
  its location and scope exactly, and do not generalise it to other
  countries or business units.
