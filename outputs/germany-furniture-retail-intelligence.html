01

Executive intelligence

Q1

Current market state

Furniture demand: contracting (4th year)Online: growing slowlyPurchase intentions: weak

Analysis German furniture is in a prolonged downturn with no recovery in sight. Retail sales fell to €25.4bn in 2025 (−1%)5. Domestic industry sales fell −4.1% in H1 2026, and the industry expects about −3% for the full year7.

Q2

What materially changed

  • 7 Sep 2026: XXXLutz formally notified the European Commission of its Porta acquisition (case M.11895, preliminary deadline 12 Oct). The Commission is also examining a possible breach of the standstill obligation1112.
  • 1 Sep 2026: the furniture industry cut its 2026 outlook to about −3%, from "at least flat"67.
  • Apr 2026: German consumers' major-purchase intentions fell to −31.5, the weakest since October 20233.
Q3

Where fulfilment activity is concentrating

Analysis Activity is concentrating in large-item (two-person handling) networks and warehouse software upgrades at group operators, not in new buildings. Examples: Otto Group's Hermes Fulfilment robots at Löhne, Poco's WMS rollout across 8 sites, and Westwing's system migration. No new German furniture DC announcement was found in the research window.

Q4

Accounts with meaningful signals

  • Otto Group / Hermes Fulfilment High Robot fleets for large items at Löhne
  • Poco (XXXLutz group) Medium WMS rollout; ERP-based warehouse control had reached its limits
  • XXXLutz / Porta · Westwing Medium Network consolidation under EU review; WMS migration
Q5

What Geek+ should monitor next

  • The Hermes Fulfilment robot rollout to Ansbach, and new fleets under Otto's coordination layer (W01)
  • The Commission's Phase 1 decision on XXXLutz/Porta by 12 Oct 2026 (W02)
  • Poco's second WMS site (end-2026) and any automation of its e-commerce fulfilment centre (W03)
  • IKEA Deutschland's FY25/26 figures and German fulfilment changes (W05)
Bottom line. Analysis The market is shrinking and consolidating, so automation activity is about efficiency inside existing networks, not new capacity. The strongest signal is at Otto Group / Hermes Fulfilment. XXXLutz is the account to watch once the Porta integration is decided.

Segment intelligence

At most three developments per segment. Evidence is Reported; "Why it matters" and "Logistics implication" are Analysis.

Large-format furniture stores

XXXLutz–Porta: consolidation under EU review

What happened
The deal was announced in January 2025 and notified to the Commission on 7 Sep 2026 (M.11895). The Commission is also examining whether the parties acted before approval1112.
Why it matters
The leading group would absorb the Porta network. Manufacturers' associations warn of buyer concentration.
Affected
XXXLutz group (incl. mömax, Poco, home24), Porta
Logistics implication
Two store-delivery and warehouse networks could be merged after clearance, which would mean network redesign rather than new build.

The retail market keeps shrinking; only kitchens grow

What happened
€25.4bn in 2025 (−1%). Kitchens +1.7%; all other categories fell5.
Why it matters
The kitchen category is project-based and built to order, while the declining categories are stock-based.
Affected
All full-range furniture stores
Logistics implication
Less stock-based volume through regional warehouses. Cost per delivery becomes the lever.

Evidence gap: IKEA Deutschland FY25 and store-chain logistics

No FY25 German results for IKEA were found; the last figures are FY249. No 2025–26 logistics announcements were found for Höffner (Krieger), Segmüller or Porta. See §9 G02 and G05.

Discount & online furniture

Poco standardises warehouse control across its network

What happened
Infios WMS went live at Trebbin as the template for 7 more sites, with the second site due by end-2026. The network has a central goods-in warehouse, regional DCs and an automated e-commerce fulfilment centre. It serves 127 stores15.
Why it matters
The previous ERP-based warehouse control had reached its limits (Reported). A standard WMS is often a precondition for further automation (Analysis).
Affected
Poco (XXXLutz group)
Logistics implication
Site-by-site go-lives through 2027 create re-design moments.

Westwing grows, but fulfilment cost rises during system migration

What happened
Q2 2026: revenue €113m (+14%), DACH +9%. Adjusted EBITDA fell to €5.4m, partly because of the move to SaaS order and warehouse management systems16.
Why it matters
This is one of the few German home-and-living online players that is growing.
Affected
Westwing
Logistics implication
With the new systems live, the next step would be productivity work in its warehouses. Their locations were not verified (G06).

Online growth is modest; a US pure-play has exited

What happened
Online furniture, lamps & décor: €5.6bn (+3.3%) in 20258. Wayfair left Germany in January 2025 and closed its Lich warehouse19 (before the research window, so context only).
Why it matters
Online share gains accrue to omnichannel incumbents such as XXXLutz/home24 and Otto.
Affected
home24, Otto, Westwing, IKEA
Logistics implication
Large-item e-commerce volume concentrates in a few existing two-person handling networks.

Manufacturing & import supply base

Domestic manufacturing keeps contracting

What happened
H1 2026 sales were €7.7bn (−2.7%), with domestic sales −4.1%. 40% of manufacturers planned short-time work in Q3. The 2026 forecast is ≈ −3%7.
Why it matters
The supply base is weakening, and capex will be minimal.
Affected
OWL furniture cluster; kitchen and upholstery makers
Logistics implication
Low likelihood of manufacturer DC investment in the near term.

Imports now supply 60% of the domestic market

What happened
Imports equal 60% of domestic sales (44% in 2019). China and Poland provide 60% of imports7.
Why it matters
More stock arrives by container and passes through import warehouses before reaching regional DCs.
Affected
Retailers with their own import hubs (Poco, XXXLutz, IKEA)
Logistics implication
Deconsolidation and storage density at import hubs become more important.

Supplier insolvencies continue

What happened
Domo Collection (upholstery, Hude) went into provisional insolvency on 3 Aug 2026, affecting ≈270 staff, most of them in Poland17.
Why it matters
It is a signal of supplier fragility. No automation relevance.
Affected
Domo Collection; its retail customers
Logistics implication
No clear automation relevance identified.

Fulfilment intelligence

Operational themes with evidence. Themes without verified evidence (returns, peak, warehouse labour) are listed in §9, not here.

Theme
Observed signal Reported
Operational implication Analysis
Distribution centres
No new German furniture DC announcement was found for Jun 2025 – Sep 2026. The existing large-item network includes Hermes Fulfilment Löhne (100,000 m²) and Ansbach13, home24 Walsrode (60,000 m², opened 2015)18 and Poco Trebbin15.
Change happens inside existing buildings, as retrofits and systems projects.
Automation
Hermes Fulfilment runs 25 Melkus pallet AMRs at Löhne (≈1,000 transports/day), with Ansbach next13. Otto's Robotic Coordination Layer uses Löhne as the blueprint for 120 sites14. Poco runs an automated e-commerce fulfilment centre (location not stated)15.
Large-item automation is moving from pilots to fleets, and orchestration across several vendors becomes a buying criterion.
Warehouse systems
Poco: Infios WMS across 8 sites15. Westwing: SaaS order and warehouse management migration completed in Q2 202616.
Once standard WMS platforms are in place, automation that integrates through standard interfaces can follow.
E-commerce fulfilment
Online furniture, lamps & décor +3.3% (2025)8. IKEA's online share in Germany is 25.1% (FY24)9. IKEA's German online orders are fulfilled partly through 3PL sites: DHL at Bergkamen (20,000 m², highly automated, opened 2022)20.
Small-parcel furnishings (décor, textiles, lighting) are the part of furniture e-commerce best suited to goods-to-person picking.
Inventory / imports
Imports equal 60% of domestic sales7. Poco combines import and regional warehouse functions at one site15.
Import hubs hold deep stock of bulky SKUs, so storage density and pallet flows matter most.
Omnichannel / consolidation
XXXLutz / Porta under EU review11. home24 is fully integrated into XXXLutz (squeeze-out March 2025, search summary)21.
Group-level network redesign is likely after clearance (Analysis).
Last mile
IKEA: 35% of German customer deliveries locally emission-free, with 300 e-vehicles (FY24)9.
Delivery is being electrified. It is a cost and ESG lever, not a warehouse-automation one.
