01

Executive intelligence

Q1

Current market state

Domestic fashion retail: flatOnline: acceleratingConsumer sentiment: well above EU

Analysis Spain's macro backdrop is healthy but domestic fashion spending is not recovering. The textile retail trade sold €11.1bn in 2025, +0.6% on 2024 and still well below 20195. The Spanish-headquartered groups are growing fast: Inditex +7.6% in H1 2026, Mango +13% in 2025710.

Q2

What materially changed

  • 30 Jul 2026: Mango began a robotics collaboration with Barcelona start-up Theker at its Lliçà d'Amunt DC11.
  • 1 Jul 2026: an EU €3 customs duty on low-value parcels took effect, affecting Shein and Temu flows into Spain14.
  • Mid-2026: construction of Inditex's second Zaragoza mega-centre (Malpica, 286,000 m²) was due for completion8.
Q3

Where fulfilment activity is concentrating

Analysis In the two home-grown global groups' own mega-DCs: Inditex in Zaragoza (Plaza + Malpica) and the Valencian Community, and Mango at Lliçà d'Amunt. These are group networks serving global demand from Spain, not domestic-market capacity. Other chains show no verified logistics activity.

Q4

Accounts with meaningful signals

No signal reaches High, because the site-level evidence is tier 3 trade press.

  • Inditex Medium Zaragoza Malpica mega-centre ramping to 1,500 jobs
  • Mango Medium AI robotics project with Theker at Lliçà d'Amunt
  • Shein / Temu Medium EU €3 parcel duty from July 2026
Q5

What Geek+ should monitor next

  • Full go-live and automation scope at Inditex Malpica (W01)
  • Scope and scale-up of the Mango–Theker robotics project (W02)
  • Tendam's logistics plans under its new owner, Multiply Group (W04)
  • Inditex FY capex split and any new Spanish logistics sites (W03)
Bottom line. Analysis Spain's fashion automation activity is concentrated in two self-built group networks with in-house engineering and start-up partnerships. The next step is to confirm the evidence at those two groups; the flat domestic market offers little new capacity demand.

Segment intelligence

Scope is fashion (apparel). There are no reliable Spain-specific luxury or accessories figures in the evidence set (§9 G07), so developments are grouped by business model. Evidence is Reported; "Why it matters" and "Logistics implication" are Analysis.

Spanish-headquartered global groups

Inditex adds mega-DC capacity in Zaragoza

What happened
The second Zaragoza centre (Malpica, 286,000 m², Zara Woman) started operating in trial mode with 250 staff in Aug–Sep 2025. Construction was due to complete in mid-2026, with 1,500 jobs at full operation89.
Why it matters
The capacity build follows a €1.8bn logistics programme in 2024–258. Inditex engineers its own automation.
Affected
Inditex (Zara)
Logistics implication
Robotised storage for hanging and boxed garments at mega scale. The equipment choices are probably already made.

Mango turns to AI robotics at its central DC

What happened
Mango began a collaboration with Theker (AI and computer-vision robotics) to raise capacity at Lliçà d'Amunt11. The site (280,000 m²) was expanded in 2021–23 with a pouch sorter, AMRs and a shipping sorter12.
Why it matters
With sales +13% and online at about ⅓, Mango is seeking more capacity from an already automated site10.
Affected
Mango
Logistics implication
The next automation step is handling complex items (piece picking), not bulk storage.

Tendam changes owner, with growth plans

What happened
Multiply Group (Abu Dhabi) completed its purchase of 67.91% of Tendam on 23 Jul 2025. It plans double-digit sales growth from 202613 (search summary).
Why it matters
A new owner with an international expansion agenda may revisit the logistics set-up.
Affected
Tendam (Springfield, Women'secret, Cortefiel, Pedro del Hierro)
Logistics implication
Tendam's logistics footprint is Unknown, so this is a watch item only.

Store-led & value chains

The domestic store network is shrinking slowly

What happened
43,452 stores (−242) in 2025. Multibrand stores fell to a 7% share, and specialised chains hold 41.3%5.
Why it matters
The market is concentrating in chains with central DCs.
Affected
Independent multibrand retailers; chains
Logistics implication
Store replenishment is concentrating in fewer, larger networks.

Primark keeps out of home delivery

What happened
In May 2026, Primark stated that its position on home delivery "has not changed". It lost the bid for the ASOS UK logistics centre to M&S15.
Why it matters
A major value player in Spain stays store-only, with no e-commerce fulfilment need.
Affected
Primark
Logistics implication
No clear automation relevance for e-commerce. Store replenishment only.

Evidence gap: El Corte Inglés and H&M Spain

A search summary describes an El Corte Inglés 2025–2030 plan targeting 30% online sales and exploring logistics uses for store space. The primary source was not opened, so it is Unconfirmed and not scored. No Spain-specific logistics news was found for H&M. See §9 G05.

Online & cross-border

Clothing is a leading online category and export

What happened
Clothing was 7% of Spanish e-commerce revenue in Q4 2025, the 2nd-largest sector, and 9.9% of outbound cross-border sales6.
Why it matters
Spanish fashion DCs ship to Europe, so their capacity is not sized for Spain alone.
Affected
Inditex, Mango, Tendam; online pure-plays
Logistics implication
High-volume single-unit picking and export parcel sorting.

An EU €3 duty applies to low-value parcels

What happened
From 1 Jul 2026, a €3 customs duty per item category applies to parcels of €150 or less14.
Why it matters
It narrows the price gap between Shein/Temu and EU-stocked fashion sellers.
Affected
Shein, Temu; Spanish value-fashion sellers
Logistics implication
It could push platforms to stock goods in the EU. No Spanish warehouse move was found.

Evidence gap: Shein's share in Spain

Tier-4 press (merca2) cites Shein at 11% of Spanish online fashion and 180m low-value parcels into Spain in 2024. The originating study was not identified, so the figures are Unconfirmed and not used. See §9 G06.

Distribution centres
Inditex Malpica, Zaragoza: 286,000 m², 113 docks, ≈€600m at full capacity89. Two Inditex warehouses in the Valencian Community (116,000 m² and 141,000 m²)9. Mango Lliçà d'Amunt: 280,000 m²12.
Capacity is concentrated in a few very large group-owned sites.
Automation
Mango: pouch sorter, AMRs and shipping sorter (2021–23 expansion; 85,000 garments/hour), plus Theker AI robotics (2026)1112. Inditex took a stake in Theker (Jul 2025) but declined to confirm deployment9.
Both groups are working with the same local AI-robotics start-up. The next frontier is piece-level handling, not bulk storage.
E-commerce fulfilment
Mango's automation cut online preparation costs by 25%12. Online is ≈⅓ of Mango sales10 and 16.9% of Spanish textile retail5.
Measured cost gains support further automation.
Returns
Mango's pouch sorter automates returns management12.
Returns are a recognised automation target, though no Spanish return-rate data was retrieved.
Last mile / parcel sorting
Paack (Madrid, 27,000 m²) sorts over 10,000 deliveries/hour with 270+ Geek+ robots and lists Inditex among its clients (2022)16.
Robotic parcel sorting for fashion e-commerce is proven in Spain at carrier level.
Cross-border
Clothing is 9.9% of Spanish outbound e-commerce6. The EU €3 duty on inbound low-value parcels applies from July 202614.
Spain is a fashion export fulfilment hub. Inbound platform flows face higher costs.

Sources & methodology

Methodology

  • Produced with the retail-research skill V0.2: report-spec.md, evidence-policy.md, visualization-guidelines.md and commercial-intelligence-framework.md.
  • Research date 28 Sep 2026. Research window for account signals: Jun 2025 – Sep 2026. Web search was used to find leads; figures are cited only from pages actually opened. Search-summary-only facts are marked Unconfirmed or excluded.
  • Chart data was downloaded from the Eurostat dissemination API (JSON-stat) on 28 Sep 2026 and embedded in the page. Chart spec blocks are in HTML comments above each chart.
  • Calculated values: period means of the retail volume indices; annual means of monthly HICP rates (CP031 vs TOTAL).
  • Acotex figures were read via FashionUnited. The Acotex definition ("comercio textil") is narrower than total fashion spending, so it is not compared with group sales.
  • Intelligence Priority scores are Analysis. They are not a sales priority, and no CRM data was used.
